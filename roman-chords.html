How to use

  1. Pick a key and a scale at the top.
  2. Type chords as Roman numerals in the big box, separated by spaces or commas, for example I V vi IV. Tap a chord under the box to add it.
  3. Press Play (or the space bar). Change the sound, style or tempo any time, even while it plays.

Reading the numerals

  • Capital letters are major chords (I IV V); lowercase are minor (ii iii vi)
  • Numerals count the notes of the chosen scale, so in A minor III is C major and VII is G major
  • Put b or # in front to move a root down or up a semitone: bVII, #iv
  • Add a suffix after the numeral: 7 maj7 6 9 add9 sus2 sus4 dim (or °) aug (or +) ø 5
  • Set Numerals mean to “Let the scale decide quality” if you want each chord’s major or minor taken from the scale instead of from capitals. Digits 1 to 7 always work this way

Try these

  • Pop: I V vi IV
  • Jazz turnaround: ii7 V7 Imaj7 Imaj7 in a major scale
  • Minor drama: i bVI bIII bVII or i iv V i in a minor scale
  • Twelve-bar blues: I7 I7 I7 I7 IV7 IV7 I7 I7 V7 IV7 I7 V7

Mood presets

  • Pick a mood, then a preset; it loads into the box in your key and sets Scale to Major with capitals meaning major, because the loops are written that way
  • Loops that start on vi sound in the relative minor: vi IV I V in C is Am F C G
  • The numbers come from 26,209 songs that have a chord chart and a mood label (Last.fm mood tags or Spotify valence and energy); each song counts once, by its main four-chord loop
  • The × figure compares how often a loop shows up in that mood with how often it shows up across all moods; above 1 means the loop leans that way
  • Indian presets rest on 85 to 148 songs per mood, so treat them as hints

Sounds and styles

  • Guitars (steel, nylon, clean electric, overdriven, 12-string, jazz archtop), pianos (grand, upright, honky-tonk, electric, harpsichord), plucked strings (ukulele, mandolin, banjo, harp, santoor), mallets (vibraphone, marimba) and sustained sounds (harmonium, organ, strings, warm pad) are all generated in your browser, so nothing downloads
  • Ukulele, mandolin and banjo play their own chord shapes, so a strum sounds like that instrument rather than a guitar
  • Strumming styles: held chord, down strums, down-up eighths, driving eighths, folk, syncopated pop, ballad, shuffle, offbeat chops, funk mutes, boom-chick, bossa nova, rolled chord, tremolo
  • Picking styles: arpeggio up, arpeggio down, arpeggio up and down, fingerpicking, Travis picking, broken chord
  • All styles work on every sound; sustained sounds hold each chord until the next one, and tremolo suits mandolin and santoor

Other controls

  • Each chord lasts: one bar, two beats or two bars in 4/4
  • Strum spread: how loose the strum is, from tight to slow
  • Room: amount of reverb
  • Humanize timing: tiny random variations so it feels played
  • Click any chord in the strip to hear it on its own

The first press of Play takes a moment while the sounds are prepared. Your settings are remembered on this device.
